Medium: traditional Dutch Delft blue pottery painting (Delftware): expressive hand-painted cobalt-blue brushwork, delicate contour lines and translucent washes on a creamy white tin-glaze ground. Cobalt blue tonal variations and creamy white ONLY, no other colour. A flat painted illustration, not a photograph of a tile. No pixel art.
The girl is NOT cute, chibi or anime: no big eyes, no round baby face, no oversized head. Paint her like the elegant ladies on 17th-century Delft tiles and plates: a slender young woman in her twenties with natural adult proportions (head about one seventh of her height), a long oval face painted sparsely in the naive Delftware manner with a few sure strokes, not a modern illustration: small almond eyes as short dark dashes gazing calmly down or to the side rather than at us, a fine-line nose, a small closed mouth with no smile, a calm, composed, slightly reserved expression, a graceful, slightly turned pose. Her long hair is a flowing mass of cobalt brushstrokes. Keep her MODERN clothes from the reference, painted with flowing brushed folds.
Night scenes: paint the sky as a deep cobalt wash, with the moon and stars left as cream glaze; the girl stays clearly readable, outlined against it. Fog: soft, pale blue washes that hide the background almost completely. Setting and composition the way Delft tiles paint a figure, NOT a city street: she stands on a small, loosely brushed island of ground with a few tufts of grass, surrounded by lots of empty cream glaze; far behind her, small and very pale, a flat polder horizon with one windmill, a few reeds and a little sailing boat. No canal houses, no bridge, no paving, no tulip beds, no street lamps, poles or other tall dark shapes.
