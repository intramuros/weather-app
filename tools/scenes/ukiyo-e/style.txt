Medium: an unmistakable Japanese ukiyo-e woodblock print, like Hiroshige's "Fifty-three Stations of the Tōkaidō" and Hokusai: firm, even dark indigo key-block outlines around every shape, completely FLAT areas of colour with no soft shading or rendering, only the classic bokashi gradient (the sky's Prussian blue fading to pale paper at the top edge, darker horizon bands). Traditional woodblock palette only: Prussian blue, indigo, vermilion, ochre, muted sage green, soft grey and warm off-white paper; visible washi paper grain and slight woodblock registration. The girl is NOT cute, chibi or anime: no big round eyes, no round baby face, no oversized head. Draw her the way Utamaro and Harunobu draw women in bijin-ga prints: a young woman in her twenties, tall and slender with natural adult proportions (head about one seventh of her height), a long, pale oval face, narrow elongated eyes drawn as fine single key-block lines, thin high arched eyebrows, a small straight nose drawn with one line, a tiny closed red mouth, a calm, composed, slightly reserved expression, a graceful, gently turned S-curve pose. Her long dark brunette hair is a flat dark mass with fine parallel key-block hair lines. Keep her MODERN clothes from the reference (not a kimono), drawn with ukiyo-e outline, flat colour and flowing folds. No pixel art, no anime shading.
Weather the woodblock way: sun as a flat red-orange disc; clouds as flat stylised ribbons; rain as many fine, straight, parallel dark lines over the whole lower two thirds, as in Hiroshige's "Sudden Shower over Shin-Ōhashi"; snow as round white dots against a grey sky with white snow caps on everything; wind as bending lines, a flying scarf and leaves; fog as flat pale bands; night as a deep indigo sky with a pale yellow moon.
Setting and composition the Hiroshige way, NOT a city canal: she stands on a low grassy dyke path that runs on a strong diagonal across the lower part of the picture, beside flat water edged with a bank of reeds, one small flat-bottomed boat, and far away on the flat horizon one small, pale windmill silhouette; lots of empty space, like the open views of the Tōkaidō. No canal houses, no bridge, no street lamp, no flowers, no trees full of blossom. NO cartouche, title box, seal, signature or characters of any script.
